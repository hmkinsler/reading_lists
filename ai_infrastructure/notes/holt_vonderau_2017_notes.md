# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The chapter opens with the authories interrogating the decision by several major tech companies to openly share and advertise their data centers, despite the fact that, as The underlying infrastructure on which cloud platforms rely, These data centers are necessarily intended to be thought of as invisible. Their chapter thus explores this tension between the supposed transparency Lauded by these technological giants and the reality that many aspects of cloud infrastructure are are still inaccessible to members of the public. They consider the politics of hypervisibility, or the way that invisible aspects of infrastructure are maintained as being out of sight by forcing attention to the aspects of technological infrastructure that these corporations feel are permissible or marketable. They argue that this obscures visibility of the material and resource costs Associated with cloud infrastructure, making their impacts difficult to map.

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
I think there's one paragraph from this text that encompasses the most key point I took away from the reading:

    "It is difficult to identify clear-cut boundaries between public and private interests in media infrastrutures, let alone in between the various businesses providing us with access to media content; nor can we assume that 'the industry' follows only one-dimensional strategic goals such as profit maximization. For instance, what we perceive as the quality and service of streamed entertainment is the effect of a complex and ever-changing web of relations that exists at a global and local, technical and social, material, and experiential levels, involving content as much as content aggregators, services as much as service providers, transport network operators as much as a mushrooming consumer media ecology. This is not anybody's industry in particular; its emergence and change can hardly be pictured in terms of one institution striving for market power. While traditional issues such as concentration of ownership, subsidies and tax breaks, operating efficiencies, and industry resources may remain useful categories for political economic analysis akin to what they were during the first wave of media mergers, today's structural convergence (and functional heterogeneity) of media in a global market demands a more case-based rather than one-size-fits-all approach" (p. 78).

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
Though published a year prior to his book, this chapter has a lot of clear resonance with Bratton (2018), especially his chapter on cloud infrastructure. The authors do a good job of explaining that cloud infrastructure cannot be conceived solely in terms of its ownership, such as pointing to a data center owned by a company like Google or Amazon, But that it should also be understood relationally in terms of the agreements that are made in various national or local context, Whether or not information is considered public or privately owned, or the protocols that are employed by different kinds of network and service providers . Here, cloud data and distribution or circulation become more topologically complex .
