# Notes for The ethnography of infrastructure (Star, S.L., 1999)

## Citation Information
Star, S.L. (1999). The ethnography of infrastructure. *American Behavioral Scientist*, *43*(3), 377-391.

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- As a seminal text in critical infrastructure studies, Star makes a "call to study boring things" (p. 377), arguing that the design of the systems that underlie infrastructure Allow us to consider the ontological questions of these design choices , a matter that is especially important given An invisible nature infrastructure as supportive substrates from which other kinds of work emerge.

- Drawing on an earlier study that she wrote with Ruhleder, she provides several dimensions of infrastructure (pp. 381-382), including:
    - Its embeddness
    - Its transparency
    - Its extensive research or scope
    - Its function as knowledge to be acquired by the members of an infrastructural community of practice
    - Its links to the conventions of practice shared by an infrastructural community
    - Its embodiment of standards
    - Its establishment on an installed base
    - Its visibility in moments of breakdown
    - Its fixing in modular increments, not at once nor globally

- She argues that an ethnographic approach to the study of infrastructure enables analysis of the relational manner in which infrastructures are established, maintained, and are made sites of work 

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- I'm still seeking to understand more the difference between an infrastructure and the communities of practice that are associated with that given infrastructure . the question I'm posing here is whether we can truly distinguish between the two? part of the challenge is that people themselves can become part of an infrastructure , such as air traffic controllers who engage in the human decision making processes on which organized air transit relies, but those same individuals have to be understood in relation to the various technologies and systems with which they engage in order to perform that work, whether through technical interfaces and dashboards, Through adherence to shared sets of protocols, and so on. In other words, how do we more clearly articulate the various factors or components that make up an infrastructure ?

- In the last five years, both in the United States and globally, we have seen a rise in environmental crises as a result of anthropogenic climate change, a rise in the deployment of surveillance technologies, increasing instances of infrastructural breakdown, etc.

Knowing this, how do we extend Star's understanding of infrastructure or the ethnography of infrastructure in a moment where infrastructural stability is becoming increasingly uncommon?

- How does the emergence of agentic AI technologies Potentially trouble or necessity further theorizing of this methodological framework? as infrastructures are increasingly being maintained and operated on the basis of agentic protocols, how do we account for the ontological consequences of those protocols in the first place and the day to day manner in which these technologies are simultaneously shaping and being shaped by society?
