# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The author interrogates his experience conducting the analysis for an ethnographic study as part of a team at Google, During which time the extrajudicial murder of George Floyd was responded to with widespread protests across the United States. He explains that the research team felt they need to grapple with issues of inequity and representation in their future endeavors

- He explains that though their intentions from the star were well meaning, an issue that he identifies as prevalent in many participatory research studies, He explains that an increased attention or focus on participants diversity did not ultimately or automatically translate to the development of a more inclusive or equitable product 

- From these limitations, he argues that an increased attention to infrastructure and infrastructural thinking is necessary for researchers to understand how they are implicated in the effects of their ethnographic research:

    "Even as ethnography is vital to the task of studying infrastructure, we have forgotten that we ourselves are imbricated within techno-social infrastructures responsible for conceptualizing, developing and maintaining a vast array of products and services. As ethnographers, our responsibilities typically centered on the ethics of the research encounter as we strive to build empathy and sensitize stakeholders and teams to the latent needs of our subjects. We wholeheartedly strive to perfect the semiotics of representation, forgetting that the forms we produce are not an end in themselves... Infrastructural thinking prompts us to find ways to decolonize not just the research encounter and modes of representation, but also the processes and sites of product-design and techno-production that follow" (pp. 225-226).

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- Given that the author of this text was working in an industry context, What lessons can be learned in terms of the ethnographic and participatory research practices that are specific to industry as opposed to academic spaces? What are some commonalities and differences in the kinds of methodological challenges faced by researchers in both of their spaces? And how does that then require for us to take up infrastructural thinking in different ways?

- Given Mendonca's point that industry researchers are culpable not only in the stakes of the research practice but also in the resulting effects of the products that they develop, how might we understand those products themselves as infrastructural?
