# Notes for Beyond representation: Using infrastructure studies to reframe ethnographic agendas and outcomes (Mendonca, K., 2022)

## Citation Information
Mendonca, K. (2022). Beyond representation: Using infrastructure studies to reframe ethnographic agendas and outcomes. *2022 EPIC Proceedings*, 220-230.

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The author interrogates his experience conducting the analysis for an ethnographic study as part of a team at Google, During which time the extrajudicial murder of George Floyd was responded to with widespread protests across the United States. He explains that the research team felt they need to grapple with issues of inequity and representation in their future endeavors

- He explains that though their intentions from the star were well meaning, an issue that he identifies as prevalent in many participatory research studies, He explains that an increased attention or focus on participants diversity did not ultimately or automatically translate to the development of a more inclusive or equitable product 

- From these limitations, he argues that an increased attention to infrastructure and infrastructural thinking is necessary for researchers to understand how they are implicated in the effects of their ethnographic research:

    "Even as ethnography is vital to the task of studying infrastructure, we have forgotten that we ourselves are imbricated within techno-social infrastructures responsible for conceptualizing, developing and maintaining a vast array of products and services. As ethnographers, our responsibilities typically centered on the ethics of the research encounter as we strive to build empathy and sensitize stakeholders and teams to the latent needs of our subjects. We wholeheartedly strive to perfect the semiotics of representation, forgetting that the forms we produce are not an end in themselves... Infrastructural thinking prompts us to find ways to decolonize not just the research encounter and modes of representation, but also the processes and sites of product-design and techno-production that follow" (pp. 225-226).

Although I can see why the author makes the connection to infrastructure, Especially for its concern with bias in classification or the embeddedness of bias and discrimination the design and development of technologies, That connection felt somewhat under theorized. ## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
the author seeks to critique research that is centered on issues of representation As a measure of diversity , which is a notable issue given that he is working from the context of industry , especially the technological corporation of Google. He essentially argues that ethnographers or participatory researchers that are seeking to respond to exclusivity or bias and discrimination in design should be careful to draw on insights from infrastructure studies to ensure that they are attentive not just to issues of representation in research design, but also to ensuring that diversity and investment in a plurality of perspectives ultimately results in processes of technological _co-design._

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
He offers three heuristics / lessons for participatory research that seeks to respond to issues of exclusivity, bias, and/or discrimination (p. 228):

    1. Think Historically: "Technological bias is historical----we cannot re-imagine and co-create futures if we remain ignorant of the ways in which it has reinvented itself within each subsequent generation of technology. A critical reading of techno-histories also provides important warnings of what could happen if these biases remain unchecked as in the failure of the British electronic industry after World War II."
    2. Measure Holistically: "Measurement can quite easily become an end unto itself. Quantitative goals are important, but they need to focus on both individual and structural accountability. The latter can be achieved by developing horizontal measures of progress that surface the dependencies between siloed initiatives."
    3. Act Cooperatively: "The work of change is complex and prone to friction. Infrastructures used to produce and maintain technology are brittle and resistant to change. Platform cooperativism offers a model of working together across functions to scale commitments and outcomes through collective action."

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
Ethnographic case study employing both quantitative / empirical methods and qualitative methods for the purpose of developing a more "diverse" persona base that would inform Google's product design.
