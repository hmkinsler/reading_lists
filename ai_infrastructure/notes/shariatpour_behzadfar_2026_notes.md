# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- As stated in the abstract, "This article interrogates whether AI necessitates new theoretical frameworks beyond smart-city paradigms by synthesising and comparing scholarship across nine interrelated dimensions--technology, governance, power, spatiality, autonomy, ethics, economy, culture and materiality" (p. 1).

- The authors look to Hangzhou's City Brain, Shanghai's AI development zone, and the Praxis Network State as three case studies for understanding how agentic AI is shaping urban societies and to formulate the basis of their calls for a new theoretic framework for agentic urban AI.

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- In what ways do the theoretical concerns raised by the authors of this article align with those raised by Tiwari (2025) and Acharya et al. (2025)?

- Given that both this article and Tiwari (2025) draw on the City Brain initiative as case studies, Where are their similarities and differences in the way that both articles conceive of that initiative as necessitating new theoretical frameworks for understanding the deployment of contemporary technologies in urban space?

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
The emphasis on governance that is the focus of this paper of course continues to tie back to Tiwari (2025) and Bratton (2016), but I'm also noticing a trend in which issues of surveillance infrastructure, Governments, and the political economy of Agentec AI technologies and systems are distinctly bound up in platform economies , as the proprietary nature of these technologies and the data that is being collected for their development and operation is such that mechanisms of control are increasingly being driven out of public infrastructure into the hands of private technological corporate owners.
