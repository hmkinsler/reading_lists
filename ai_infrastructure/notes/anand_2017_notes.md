# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The majority of this blog post from Anand focuses on the infrastructural failure that led to the lead contamination of The water systems for the city of Flint, Michigan and the sentiment shared by many residents in Mumbai That their lack of access to water infrastructure was an indication That they were not being recognized as true citizens of their city. 

- In alignment with the argument outlined in Star (1999), Anand explains that infrastucture can be politically dangerous precisely because of its banal nature, operating on a logic that makes infrastructure seem unimportant or uninteresting and thus not worth addressing, even when infrastructural failures are resulting in material harm, especially in a way that disproportionately effects marginalized populations.

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- Anand criticizes the willful ignorance that enables a lack of action or response to infrastructural failure. in the context of emerging AI technologies however, which are known to be prone to failures of their own in the form of hallucinations, How do we contend with the errors or negative effects of such technology is in an instance where their developers may claim that AI failures are not the result of their lack of interest but are more so an inherent aspect of the technology itself?

In particular, I'm thinking of a lawsuit between Robbie Starbuck and Google, the former of whom alleged that the Google AI summary tool that is integrated into their search engine inaccurately asserted that he had perpetrated sexual assault and was a white nationalist, a claim that Google refutes on the basis that they should not be responsible for failures on the part of users to identify hallucinated information , as these errors are inherent to AI technologies. How do such legal disputes potentially complicate the idea that infrastructure is banal in instances where the developers of a given infrastructural system or technology are seeking new avenues to absolve themselves of responsibility for the resulting effects of said technologies?
