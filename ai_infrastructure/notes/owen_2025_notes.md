# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- A bit of a unique addition to my reading list, as this source is a web text with a variety of distinct pages that readers can navigate through, Owen's site is a "gallery" through the various layers that make up the infrastructure of AI technologies:

    - Extract: Beginning with a focus on the materiality of computing technologies, the first layer of the site surveys scholarship concerned with technological obsolescence and the ever increasing need for rare earth minerals to be extracted to support the hardware on which this technology runs, Case studies include looking at cobalt mines in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and tin mines in Indonesia.

    - Produce: This layer considers how the raw materials that make up computing hardware are processed And manufactured, Looking at production sites in China, Taiwan, And the United States as case studies.

    - Transport: This layer explores the logistic infrastructures that enable the transportation of raw and processed materials alike, Considering the ports, canals, water and airway is by which these materials are circulated. The transportation of AI computing materials is considered both in terms of the geopolitical dynamics that drive technological economies, as well as in terms of environmental consequences of the carbon emissions associated with the transportation of these materials. 

    - Connect: Here, Owen explores the massive scale on which data is extracted, stored, and transmitted in order to enable the development of AI models and technologies. He considers multiple routes through which data transmission occurs, such as via GPS and satellite, through fiber optic cables, And via data centers. 

    - Compute: Building on discussion in the previous layer, Owen further explores the consequences of the data centers that serve as the cloud infrastructure for AI technologies, which have links to the United States' Department of Defense as a key stakeholder in data center development. Owen considers the ecological consequences and resource extraction required to maintain data centers.

    - Train: For the final layer, Owen considers the labor that is required in order to enable the training of AI models, especially through the outsourcing of this training labor From the northern and western contexts in which technological corporations typically operate to communities in the Global South.

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
This web text is similar in some ways to Crawford's attempt to compile A reference guide or atlas for understanding AI technologies. In this case, owen is largely focused on the infrastructure is that underlie and sustain these technologies, with several explicit references to Bratton's (2016) theoretical model of the Stack. in this case, Owen is offering a model for a stack of his own, With a specific focus on how AI technologies reach the scale of planetary computation.

He mentions a few specific and compelling points about the materiality of microelectronics and computing chips, Such as the increasing use of tin as opposed to lead based solder. He also includes a number of cities or sites that act as case studies for various kinds of AI infrastructure, including port cities that act as logistical connective hubs that transport computing materials , hyperscale data centers that are used to store massive amounts of digitized information, and sites of mineral and material extraction including within the Democratic Republic of Congo.
