### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
The overall take away from this article for me is that to understand contemporary AI technologies means that we have to be more specific in defining exactly what "AI" means. At one point, Their analysis includes a surveying of the technical documentation that's associated with each of the three platforms' cloud offerings, some of which include terms like "'AI', 'ML', and 'Deep Learning'" (p. 7) specifically. Not all tools with this documentation were titled as "AI" technologies, however, though they could be clearly identified by researchers as making use of AI technologies. From my perspective, given that this documentation is targeted toward a more technical audience, likely engineers, developers, and programmers, it makes sense that the term "AI" would carry slightly different meanings, potentially to the degree of being contextually or industry specific. At a time where technological development is unfolding so rapidly, to the point that the very design of data centers and chips is shifting to be more suited for machine learning and AI use cases, It seems to follow that analysis of AI technologies in both the humanities and social sciences needs to be more attentive to the specific context in which those technologies are being deployed.

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship

This is also a bit of an aside about this reading, but I did think it was kind of neat and also a little bit funny and ironic that they developed their own custom web scrapers for pulling information from the technical documentation for all of these cloud services and products from these major technological corporations. ## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
They seek to provide a more detailed analysis or interrogation of major technological corporations to help support the claim that "there is no AI without Big Tech" (p. 1).

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
The authors essentially illustrate the infrastructural interdependent nature of AI technological development and the resources and labor that are accessible at scale only to the largest technological corporations. In other words, they seek to illustrate how the contemporary technological market has been defined by the emergence of monopolies, And as a result, contemporary AI technologies themselves have been defined by their dependence on these major corporations.

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
The researchers make use of various public facing texts and documentation related to the various cloud products of three major technological corporations in cloud platforms, including Microsoft, Google, and Amazon. These texts include things like "technical documentation, developer and product websites, financial reports, media articles, press releases, and blog posts that provide detailed insights into the intended objectives, functions, and capabilities of technological systems" (p. 4).

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
- Technographic analysis

"Technography encourages a detailed examination of the material aspects of technology by directly reading various publicly available documents generated by and related to technical systems. These documents include technical documentation, developer and product websites, financial reports, media articles, press releases, and blog posts that provide detailed insights into the intended objectives, functions, and capabilities of technical systems. This enables us to explain specific functionalities or aspects of the technology while maintaining a critical stance toward promotional language or industry jargon found in these materials" (p. 4).

## Qualifying Exam Relevance
Overall, the key issue at hand in this article is that AI and cloud infrastructure are becoming increasingly privatized, which poses issues for ensuring equitable and responsible governance of AI tools, systems, and technologies, which are increasingly being handled via major technological corporations' platforms, including Google, Amazon, and Microsoft. The three primary drivers of this concentration of resources and infrastructure into the hands of major tech corporations includes:

1. Proprietary integration of hardware and software
2. AI's widespread use across many sectors, serving as the infrastructure for a multitude of other systems
3. Various dependences along the AI chain, including in computational resources and cost
