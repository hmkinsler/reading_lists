# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article focuses on tracing the development and deployment of Agentic AI systems and technology specifically in urban contexts , considering how the deployment of such systems carries various social affordances and risks, and the consequences of these technologies for Geopolitical Theory. The paper focuses on two agentic AI platforms as case studies, including Alibaba's City Brain and Citymind AI Agent, Both of which he argue are evidence of A recent shift toward integration of AI technologies into urban space, "compelling a transformative shift in governance" (p.1).

- He offers theoretical framework or definition of agentic urban AI, Considering how this has emerged out of planning and development of autonomous cities, smart cities, and other planning and urban management strategies that seek to develop hybrid physical/digital spaces. He also distinguishes between automation, autonomy, and agency in urban AI systems, offering some conceptual clarity on the degree to which humans are "in the loop" of these systems.

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- The article's title suggests that agentic AI technologies are distinct, by virtue of their ability to shift from "automation to agency" (p. 1). Given this, How is the author defining agency beyond the ability to act autonomously?

- The article's abstract also alludes to shifts in urban governance resulting from the recent deployment of Agentic AI technologies in city space. Here, Where are their points of alignment or divergent from the manner in which Benjamin Bratton discusses the city layer of The Stack? In other words, how do we account for the emergence of AI technologies, and especially agentic AI, in the continual (re)development of urban space within the context of planetary scale computation?

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
The author here seems interested in defining the aspects that make agentic AI technologies distinct from their earlier autonomous AI counterparts, the former of which is most characteristically defined bye the ability to independently set and manage its own goals. Tiwari acknowledges that the deployment of Agentic AI technologies in urban spaces is emergent, but he sees some of the risks associated with these technologies as severe enough to warrant anticipatory regulatory action and ethical analysis before the shift from existing "smart city" models to post-smart urbanism fully takes hold.

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
There are a lot of clear connections between issues of governance that are raised in this article to those that are so predominant in Bratton's (2016) analysis. In particular, Bratton's discussion of the city layer of the stack has the clearest alignment here-- interest in how agentic AI as a form of computation leads to new forms of urban spatiality, urban governance, and sovereignty.

This link between governance, geopolitics, and spatiality seems to be a really common theme for critical urban studies , especially in the context of analyzing recent or emergent technologies. It is notable to me that this article and its close counterpart in Shariatpour & Behzadfar (2026) Have both been published so recently, and the concerns that are outlined in both of their articles align with my own observations pertaining to the deployment of surveillance infrastructure or surveillance technologies in my own city .

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
- Agentic AI is unique in its ability to direct and manage its own goals, marking a divergence from previous smart-city models characterized by human-directed, autonomous AI technologies

- "Scholars have warned that the deployment of intelligent technologies frequently outpaces the development of inclusive and reflexive governance mechanisms...
