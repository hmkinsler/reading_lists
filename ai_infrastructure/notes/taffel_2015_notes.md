### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Taking up scholarship in materialist media studies, the author Analyzes legislative action and the release of specially designed phones as attempts to mitigate harm caused by ongoing conflict Related to minerals including Colton, Tantalum, tungsten, tin, and gold in the Democratic Republic of Congo. in particular, He makes use of media colleges and object oriented ontology as the theoretical frameworks for Mapping the various contextual factors or global flows of capital and minerals that are enmeshed in this extractive cycle. Ultimately, the author Argues that such theoretical perspectives are particularly apartment, and thus necessary, for fully interrogating the political and material economy from which technologies are materialized: 

    "A central contention of this paper has been that the claim of dematerialisation inherent in numerous discourses surrounding digital culture is fundamentally misplaced; far from being a system of free-floating information in a virtual cyber-reality, the internet is thin A material technological system like any other, albeit one whose specific contemporary forms, especially those surrounding wireless information transmission, thin-clinet access to networks and the miniaturisation of electronics, present a seductive appearance that materiality somehow marginalized. However, when interrogating the ethics and politics of media systems, it is crucial to include analyses of the flows of matter and energy, the assemblages of minerals, metals, human labour, and toxic by-products, without which there would be no internet" (p. 28).
