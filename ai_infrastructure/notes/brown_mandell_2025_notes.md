# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The authors outline their sticks for a feminist digital Humanities infrastructure , one that seeks to resist a binary Gender essentialism that classifies people into the categories of either female or male, considering how a feminist perspective could inform several matters of concern for the digital humanities and critical infrastructure studies, including:
    - Critiques of The Stack (Bratton, 2016)
    - Interrogation Of the labor on which infrastructure has been developed and continues to be maintained and rely on
    - Troubling the implementation of standards or classification systems that, by necessity, homogenize or essentialize the things that are meant to be described within that system or any given classification within it
    - Accounting for the materiality of infrastructure, Especially as it often relies on processes of extraction and the geopolitical economy of materials
    - Investigation of the ontological politics of software design , which can both perpetuate systems of oppression and also bring forth feminist alternatives through design
    - Outline of the principles for feminist interface design 
    - Promote feminist perspectives on the relational nature of infrastructure 

Ultimately, they call for a feminist infrastructural ecosystem, or feminist digital humanities infrastructure that, they write, is "best framed theoretically, we have argued, not as a stack, but as an ecosystem in which actors and activities interrelate and intra-act, Impacting each other in the context of local environments that themselves differ remarkably... A feminist DH Infrastructure aims to redistribute power in order to achieve equity and empowerment. It consequently embraces pluralism: identity, inclusivity, and diversity. It respects all participants as agents, giving credit where it is due and making labor visible" (p. 91).

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- Though digital humanities is a broad interdisciplinary field, that in many respects has been difficult to cleanly define or encapsulate, How do the authors think about issues of infrastructure that are particularly relevant to digital humanities work? In other words, Why do the authors place an emphasis on digital infrastructure and digital humanities scholarship as opposed to infrastructure more broadly or humanities research more broadly? What is at stake for a feminist infrastructural approach to digital humanities in particular, and what could be taken from this theoretical perspective for scholars in the humanities that are not solely focused on digital work?

- How did the authors point to a kind of infrastructural futurity, or an intentional building of new kinds of infrastructural arrangements?
