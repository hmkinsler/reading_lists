### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Though a very brief article, Suchman seeks to interrogate The discursive tendency of scholars to describe AI as a singular entity, or "thing", writing "my argument is that the thingness of AI, its status as a stable and a gentle entity, needs to be made controversial: that we need to prioritize critical engagement with the work being done by the figure of AI in specific contexts. To let the term pass is to miss the opportunity to trace its sources of power into demystify its referents" (p. 1).

- In other words, She is making the call for descriptions of AI that are always contextualized, whether for AI's role in a broader history of computational and cognitive science, Its use as both a technique and technology, or braoad signifier that is linked to flows of social, political, and economic capital.

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
Though this article is only a few pages long, It definitely struck me as one of the most needed interventions into ongoing discussion about" AI" in the humanities and social sciences. Here, suchman is especially interested in how the term AI is taken up by scholars in so many broad or undefined ways that it becomes this uncontroversial thing that has a multitude of discourses or controversies surrounding it without there being enough explicit attention to what the meaning of AI is in a specific context that will, by extension, Have specific social, cultural, political, and material consequences. In other words, She is critiquing the tendency of scholars to use the term AI to refer to both contemporary large language models and front facing interfaces in the form of tools like ChatGpt or Claude while also using that term in reference to some of the earliest natural language processing technologies like ELIZA, or even using the term artificial intelligence to refer to new modes of computational work in the form of neural networks. To fail to make these contextual specifications, she argues, then directs attention away from the design choices that are specific to each of these iterations of AI technologies, Which then obscures the hegemonic onto epistemologies that shape their development, circulation , and use. Ultimately, If I were to pick a singular text that I see as the most important for anybody to read about understanding the current state of discourse in academy about AI technologies, tools, and systems, It would be this article.
