# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
#### Introduction (2nd Edition)
- Rethinking the relationship between humans amd technological machines, especially in the context of posthumanism or new materialist perspectives on the issue of _agency_
- Explains relationship between the first and second editions of this text

#### Readings and Responses (2nd Edition)
Offers responses and reflections on how the first edition of the book was received.

#### Preface (1st Edition)
Includes several epigraphs from Ingold (2000) and Bowers (2002) to help frame the book's focus on practice, embodiment, and action, especially as she cites these as two key texts that were critical in informing her own writing process for this text. 

#### Introduction (1st Edition)
She frames the goal of the book as exploring "the conception of purposeful action, and consequently of interaction, informing the design of interactive machines" (p. 30). In other words, she seeks to explore scholarship on the planning processes of human action, which she then interrogates to offer an argument for a distinction between plans and situated actions. 

#### Interactive Artifacts (1st Edition)
She considers how computational artifacts are characterized by their "_reactive_, _linguistic_, and internally _opaque_ properties" (p. 35) that afford or enable interaction, especially as evident in the work of researchers seeking to both understand and be understood by their machine counterparts. She traces the development of cognitive science as a disciplinary area to understand how human and machine intelligence have been understood relative to each other, and the consequeces of how machine intelligence or capability as been understood in shaping human-computer interaction. 

#### Plans
Here, Suchman begins to sketch her argument that scholarship on interaction must take up a clear distinction between _plans_ and _situated actions,_ the former of which is the focus of this chapter. She states that cognitive science planning models of human action are more predominant in AI research perspectives, though the model is limited for its reliance on rationalist, cognitivist, and behaviorist perspectives. She writes, "the model posits that action is a form of problem solving, where the actor's problem is to find a path from some initial state to a desired goal state, given certain conditions along the way" (pp. 52-53). Essentially, an actor's goals are of the greatest theoretical importance, as the conditions that a user encounters are merely effects that will enable or prevent achievement of said goals.

#### Situated Actions
In contrast, a concept of _situated actions_ enables a more contextual understanding of action, especially as it "underscored the view that every course of action depends in essential ways on its material and social circumstances" (p. 70). The implication, then, is that actions are never fully or concretely planned, as they have to be understood in the context that shapes how they actually unfold.

#### Communicative Resources
As an example of the points made in the previous two chapters, she considers how a planning model for communication, especially which she identifies as characteristic of speech act theory, differs from a more dynamic perspective on conversation as "ensemble work" (p. 87), or in analysis of how face-to-face conversation unfolds situationally.

#### Case and Methods
As a precursor to the analysis of the _expert help system_ in the following chapter, this chapter introduces the case study, including the design and intended purpose of the system, and methodological issues related to how instructions are relayed to participants in research.

#### Human-Machine Communication
This chapter focuses on analytical findings from the _expert help system_ case study, especially regarding "the consequences of taking a statement of intent and an ascribed plan as grounds for the interpretation of situated action" (p. 127).

#### Conclusion (1st Edition)
Summarizes the key finding of the book, namely in offering an alternative to cognitivist models of human planning and action and sketching out a methodological / theoretical framework for understanding human-machine communication and interaction based on this more situated model.

#### Plans, Scripts, and Other Ordering Devices (2nd Edition)
Considers how the distinction between plans and situated actions has been taken up in human-computer interaction and human-machine communication research since the book's initial release.

#### Agencies at the Interface (2nd Edition)
Considers how agency can be understood when investigated at the site of the interface, especially in the context of software or tangible interfaces as seen in the case of wearable technologies. Here, Suchman considers how software helps to invisibilize certain aspects of computing infrastructure, especially as informed by ubicomp discourses of innovation.

#### Figuring the Human in AI and Robotics (2nd Edition)
Explores "the question of what conceptions of the human inform current projects in AI and robotics" (p. 5).

#### Demystifications and Reenchantments of the Humanlike Machine (2nd Edition)
Considers the impacts and implications of recent developments in ubicomp.

#### Reconfigurations (2nd Edition)
Building on the previous chapter, explores in a speculative manner how human-machine interaction and relations might be imagined otherwise.

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
- Responding to cognitivist and behaviorist perspectives that frame plans as preceding and being determinant of actions, especially as something internal to a given actor's mental state

- Her second edition, especially, builds on the original 1987 of Suchman's _Plans and Situated Actions_, especially to consider the implications of her original work in the wake of technological development, growing interest in autonomous or agentic AI technologies, and the rise of posthumanist or new materialist perspectives in science and technologies studies

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
- "I take the boundaries between persons and machines to be discursively and materially enacted rather than naturally effected and to be available, for better and worse and with greater and lesser resistances, for refiguring. It is precisely because the distinction between person and machine rests on the traffic back and forth between the two terms that questions of human-machine identity and difference matter" (p. 12).

- Introduces concept of _situated action_: "every course of action depends in essential ways on its material and social circumstances" (p. 70).

- "Rather than build a theory of action out of a theory of plans, the aim is to investigate how people produce and find evidence for plans in the course of situated action. More generally, rather than subsume the details of action under the study of plans, plans are subsumed by the larger problem of situated action" (p. 70).

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
- Audio-visual analysis of two novice users attempting to use the "expert help system" to complete various tasks with a Xerox print and copy machine

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
- Interrogates cognitivist models of human planning
- Interrogation of speech act theory
- Ethnomethodology
- Conversation analysis and face-to-face communication studies
- Lots of ties to the work of Goodwin and colleagues in understanding the situated nature of conversational interaction
- Ends the book with a move toward understanding the complicated implications of various conceptions of human-machine relations and agency for STS
