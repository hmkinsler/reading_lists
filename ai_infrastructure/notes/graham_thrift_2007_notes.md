# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The authors Focus their article on exploring how processes of maintenance and repair are employed in major cities as a key point of their infrastructural sustainment. They outline several sections of the article as follows:

    " In the first section we will make the theoretical case for the humble but vital processes of repair and maintenance as the 'ready' of 'ready-to-hand'. In the subsequent sections we will turn towards the key manifestation of social systems of maintenance and repair, namely modern cities. Thus the second section inquires into why the business of maintenance and repairing cities has so often been neglected. The third and fourth sections provide examples of particular sub-systems of maintenance and repair, namely electricity supply, and particularly its intersection with information and communications technology (ICT), And automobility Then, in the concluding part of the article, we will argue that repair and maintenance activities have not just more grip but more emancipatory potential than may be thought by those who want to write them off as simply mundane or slavishly repetitive" (p. 2).

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- In their description of the conclusion of the article, the authors state that those who see infrastructure as banal or mundane should heed to a call to action to see the emancipatory potential of repair and maintenance . At the same time, It seems to be the case that there are different perspectives on the banality of infrastructure depending on who is providing analysis on it's affordances and limitations. Given this, how do we understand the difference between banal perspectives versus emancipatory perspectives on infrastructure based on the power of the individual(s) making that assessment?

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
They argue that there are three things that would be made more visible for theorizing and analysis if greater attention were given to maintenance and repair:

    1. Decay
    2. "...the way in which maintenance and repair can itself be a vital source of variation, improvisation, and innovation" (p. 6)
    3.  "...the sheer amount of economic activity -- usually ignored in accounts of 'global' cities -- generated by repair and maintenance" (p. 7)

In their exploration of processes of maintenance and repair in urban contexts, They explore four points that they feel illustrate how, paradoxically, These processes are superficially and frequently discussed in urban studies scholarship while simultaneously not being given the analytical or theoretical depth and attention that Graham and Thrift call for:

    1. "First, the very fact that certain technosocial complexes emerge that are deemed to be 'infrastructure' often works to undermine their cultural visibility" (p. 8)
    2. "Second, attention to the need for repair and maintenance of infrastructures tends only to occur after catastrophic, rather than prosaic failures" (p. 9)
    3. "Third, the 'black boxing' of infrastructural systems, and the failure of their users to see beyond the flowing tap, the car ignition, the computer screen, the telephone handset or the burning stove, to the empire of functions 'behind' the working service, has further important implications for the imaginations of urban infrastructure" (p. 10)
    4. "The final reason for the neglect of cities of repair is the continuing failure to satisfactorily incorporate global South urbanism within the dominant discourse of 'urban studies'" (p. 11)

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
Out of the readings in this list, there is the clearest alignment with the argument made in Jackson (2014).

There are also a lot of points throughout the reading that connect Two issues within urban studies scholarship, and I noted several instances in which I especially felt like this reading connects to Anand's (2017) description of the finality of infrastructure, or the way in which their power asymmetries that dictate who calls attention to infrastructural failures , and the tendency of people or entities and positions of power to deny these failures so as to deny necessity of reparative action.

What especially stood out to me, however, with their discussion of black boxing, Which surprisingly seemed to connect quite well to literature in both media studies and rhetoric and composition studies about pedagogical issues For critical digital or technological literacy. From my perspective, at a time when infrastructural decay is so visible and apparent in the United States, partially because of the banality that Anand describes, We face long term issues pertaining to enough people are being trained and gaining the kind of technical expertise in literacy that is necessary in order to successfully perform repairs of these systems. Just as Jackson points to the diminishing attention to our personal traditions or craft traditions, Increasing assumptions that these maintenance and repair processes can merely be automated help to legitimize a lack of resource allocation to ongoing training and preparation for the Installation, Maintenance, and Repair (IMR) workforce.

This seems like a site where there is a lot of very obvious connections to the problems that could be posed in terms of this recent acceleration in the development of AI technologies, and especially agentic AI.

Also, there are lots of connections in their discussion of the electrical grid to Bennett (2005, 2010).
