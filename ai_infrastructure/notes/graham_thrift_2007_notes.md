### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
They argue that there are three things that would be made more visible for theorizing and analysis if greater attention were given to maintenance and repair:

    1. Decay
    2. "...the way in which maintenance and repair can itself be a vital source of variation, improvisation, and innovation" (p. 6)
    3.  "...the sheer amount of economic activity -- usually ignored in accounts of 'global' cities -- generated by repair and maintenance" (p. 7)

In their exploration of processes of maintenance and repair in urban contexts, They explore four points that they feel illustrate how, paradoxically, These processes are superficially and frequently discussed in urban studies scholarship while simultaneously not being given the analytical or theoretical depth and attention that Graham and Thrift call for:

    1. "First, the very fact that certain technosocial complexes emerge that are deemed to be 'infrastructure' often works to undermine their cultural visibility" (p. 8)
    2. "Second, attention to the need for repair and maintenance of infrastructures tends only to occur after catastrophic, rather than prosaic failures" (p. 9)
    3. "Third, the 'black boxing' of infrastructural systems, and the failure of their users to see beyond the flowing tap, the car ignition, the computer screen, the telephone handset or the burning stove, to the empire of functions 'behind' the working service, has further important implications for the imaginations of urban infrastructure" (p. 10)
    4. "The final reason for the neglect of cities of repair is the continuing failure to satisfactorily incorporate global South urbanism within the dominant discourse of 'urban studies'" (p. 11)
