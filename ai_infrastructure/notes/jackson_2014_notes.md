# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Jackson Surveys several themes of maintenance and Repair Oregon intervention into breakdown over the course of this chapter, especially to consider how repair is often placed in contradiction to technological obsolescence or discourses of innovation. Furthermore, he considers how or pair is bound up in issues of power and knowledge, asking Whether the ability to repair offers a particular kind of knowledge and thus access to power, and what the implications are in epistemology of repair. He further considers how repair and maintenance are bound up in an ethics of care, or a moral standpoint that holds that ecological care and attention to technology Aid in resisting discourses legitimize further material extraction. Ultimately, he argues that these themes Require greater attention in technology studies:

    These three themes--innovation, knowledge/power, and the ethics of care-- Constitute missing elements or dimensions of the way we think in new media and technology studies typically about breakdown, maintenance, and repair (when we think about it at all). But they also raise new challenges and opportunities in the study of technology more generally, Some of which connect to the very old problem of how to frame a more humane and progressive politics of technology (p. 233).

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
I think what I valued the most from this chapter was his interest in an epistemology of repair and whether the field of science and technology studies or media studies can fully account for the standpoint epistemology is those who engage in the labor of maintenance and repair . This has been an ongoing question for me my own critical making work and scholarship, as it still tends to be the case in most academic institutions that text based forms of scholarship take priority, so to make a critically made piece legible as a publication or account toward 10 year review and so on and so forth, one is required to fit the conceptual, theoretical, and conceptual work that corresponds with that piece into a textual mode.

This of course is not an issue that is really of note for this particular reading list, but it was a point that certainly stood out to me from this chapter and it's something I would be interested in coming back to potentially when it comes to working on my dissertation .

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
there is a lot of very clear resonance between this chapter and the chapter from Graham and Thrift, But I also found it notable that Jackson alludes to some of the issues that feature more predominantly in some of his later scholarship, namely in confronting the problem of technoliberalism, dominant discourses of innovation, and the corresponding maker practices that perpetuate Cycles of technological obsolescence and waste. Felt as though this chapter was planting some of the seeds for the concerns that he raises his Co authored pieces on Human Computer interaction and critical making .

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
There are a few specific instances or potential case studies that Jackson brings up to illustrate how fundamental maintenance and repair processes are to sustaining infrastructure, especially computational infrastructure, but I was especially struck by his discussion of the design choice is made by Apple for several of their products that reduce users ability to make repairs or replace parts on their own. When there was public outcry and response from the user or consumer base in response to these changes , the company argued that repair is lots of concern for the vast majority of consumers today And that there was more of an interest in functionality or other kinds of design affordances.

Although Jackson does not specifically speak to the politics of right to repair explicitly, he definitely alludes to several of these issues in the evidence that he provides.
