# Patterns from previous CRDM exam questions
- Instructions to synthesize and put multiple readings into conversation with one another, considering where they may converge or diverge, how a disciplinary conversation changes over time, or provide models for mapping out a disciplinary conversation that is taking place.
- Draw on readings to apply to a given case study.
- Explain how the readings are informing your proposed research for the dissertation, especially to defend the feasability, theoretical framework, and methodology you intend to engage in your work, as well as what you see as the intervention or contribution your research will make to the field.
- Compare and contrast between theoretical perspectives from multiple scholars, offering synthesis or your own argumentative perspective in response.
- Define major theories, clarify conceptual definitions, and identify key themes covered in the readings.
- Identify limitations and gaps or areas for future research in a given disciplinary area based on your readings.
- Extrapolate and explain some of the choices made in writing your exam rationales.
- Describe several key studies from the reading lists that you believe are exemplars of this scholarship and clearly explain what they do well, methodologically and theoretically.

# Considerations for exam process
- Ensure that there is access to voice-to-text technologies to assist with writing
- Identify which writing software(s) or any other writing materials will be useful to include for the written exams

# Approaches for note taking
- Develop thematic maps to help synthesize the research you are reading-- you might develop groups by topic, theoretical / methodological approach, or historical debates unfolding in the field
- Continuously tie back the readings to your research, asking how they inform or intersect with your proposed topic
- Create your own questions based on the lists and practice answering them without notes
- Draw on note-taking models that aid in tracking the major arguments of each source (i.e. SQR3, intervention / evidence / argument)
- Even when focusing on a specific chapter(s) from a book, you should briefly read through and skim through the rest of the text, especially the introduction and conclusion sections, to understand where the argument that the author(s) hone in on within that specific section are situated in relation to the broader context of the text
