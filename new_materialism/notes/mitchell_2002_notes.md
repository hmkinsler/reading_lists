# Notes for Can the mosquito speak? (Mitchell, T., 2002)

## Citation Information
Mitchell, T. (2002). Can the mosquito speak?. In *Rule of experts: Egypt, techno-politics, modernity* (pp. 1-27). University of California Press.

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This chapter illustrates how a new materialist perspective Can be employed for the purposes of conducting historical research . In this case, Mitchell is offering a critical historical analysis of the effects of mosquitoes In the context of the malaria outbreaks that started in Egypt in the mid 20th century. 

- Throughout the chapter, Mitchell considers the various forces that culminated in the outbreak , How it could not be considered as a historical event that occurred in isolation. For example, He explains how regional conflict and the impacts of colonial occupation led to infrastructural mismanagement and resource disputes in Egypt that facilitated widespread malnutrition and greater susceptibility to disease.

- He explains that the case study of the malaria outbreak in Egypt offers several lessons for Interrogating the norms that undergird social theory and the social sciences, including the tendency to analyze historical events as though they are local instantiations of broader social patterns or processes, as well as an overemphasis on the roll and status of humans in shaping historical events .

- To exemplify these pitfalls in social theory, Mitchell takes up as a case study The business ventures of Ahmad Abbud, an Egyptian landowner and capitalist, tracing his role in as a facilitator of the spread of mosquitoes and malaria through analysis of the various other agentive forces he interfaced with.

- He ends the chapter by emphasizing that his goal is not To equate human agency with non human agency or equate all kinds of networks at play in the unfolding of historical events. Instead, he argues that an investment in nonhuman agencies enables new ways to theorize or contextualize historical events, writing:

    Instead of invoking the force and logic of reason, self-interest, science, or capital and attributing what happens in the world to the working of these enchanted powers and processes, we can open up the question, as I have attempted here, of what kinds of hybrid agencies, connections, interactions, and forms of violence are able to portray their actions as history, as human expertise overcoming nature, as the progress of reason and modernity, or as the expansion and development of capitalism.

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
"The chain is in fact more than a triangle. The connections between a war, an epidemic, and a famine depended upon connections between rivers, dams, fertilizers, food webs, and, as we will see, several additional links and interactions. What seems remarkable is the way the properties of these various elements interacted. They were not just separate historical events affecting one another at the social level. The linkages among them were hydraulic, chemical, military, political, etiological, and mechanical. No one writing about Egypt in this period describes this interaction. There are studies of military tactics, irrigation methods, Anglo-Egyptian relations, hydraulic engineering, parasites, the sugar industry, and peasants. But there are no accounts that take seriously how these elements interact. It is as if the elements are somehow incommensurable. They seem to involve very different forces, agents, elements, spatial scales, and temporalities. They shape one another, yet their heterogeneity offers a resistance to explanation.

The resistance may have something to do with the mixing of natural and social worlds..." (p. 7).
