### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The chapter starts off with Barad interrogating issues that arise from the primacy Of language and linguistic representation following the linguistic turn. here, she makes the argument that the theory of performativity allows us to understand how materiality is made secondary to language and representationalism. Here, she states that not only do post structuralists help to reveal these issues, but so do the theory is of Niels Bohr . Given this, she states, "in this chapter, I propose a _posthumanist performative_ approach to understanding technoscientific and other naturalcultural practices that specifically acknowledges and takes accounts of matter's dynamism" (p. 135)

- She describes her perspective as an "agential realist ontology" (p. 136)

- She traces the development of Neil's Bo indeterminate model of the atom , which she states requires a new ontoepistemological framework: "This account refuses the representationalist fixation on words and things and the problematic of the nature of the relationship, advocating instead _a relationality between specific material reconfigurings of the world through which boundaries, properties, and meanings are differentially enacted_ (i.e., discursive practices, in my posthumanist sense) _and specific material phenomena_ (i.e., differentiating patterns of mattering)" (p. 139).

- It is on this basis that she proposes the concept of _intra-action_

- In one section, she interrogates the meaning of _apparatus_

- She then shifts into _mattering_

- Issues of _bodily boundaries_ in the context of a posthumanist and relational ontology

- And likewise, the boundaries of an apparatus

- And the consequences of this in determining the boundaries between humans and objects or subjects, The consequences for this in terms of an Objectivist ontology, and how we understand separation between different agential bodies

- Finally, She considers the implications of this kind of approach for models of determinacy or causality 

- overall, The vast majority of the chapter is focused on tracing the development of quantum theoretical models, primarily the work of Niels Bohr, and the consequences of these more indeterminate explanations for physics and reality for humanist ontologies
