### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
In this case this article raised a lot more questions for me than it did answers. Essentially, ahmed's argument is that new materialist scholarship , as part of the argument for its disciplinary necessity, has framed prior feminist scholarship as overly preoccupied with language, discourse, and representation, to the detriment of materialist and biological analysis. Ahmed thus assesses a wide range of feminist scholarship that identifies itself as explicitly concerned with biology, particularly in its implications for understanding women's lives and lived experiences. She rightfully points out that critiques of biological essentialism on the part of feminists can be misconstrued as a denial of biology rather than recognizing that these scholars are simply rejecting that particular model of biology that mistakenly attributes all social behavior to biology, and she seems to be suggesting that part of this issue that she has identified in the founding gestures of new materialist scholarship may arise out of that reduction or misinterpretation of that work. At the same time, she does also point out that there is quite a bit of a focus on biology in posthumanism, which does raise the question as to why new materialist scholars are situating their focus as different.

She also offers some commentary or critique of the idea that feminist scholarship is overly focused on culture and reducing everything to culture . In her words, "given the concern with the social reproduction of hierarchies, much feminist work might point to the complexity of the relationship between materiality and culture, rather than reducing one to the other" (p. 33).

I have some mixed feelings when this article overall because it is not clear to me yet the degree to which feminist poststructuralist scholarship maintains much more focused and concrete view of materiality as opposed to a focus on representation and discourse. There are other scholars who have raise this point as well, Latour offers some commentary on semiotics, for example:

"The weakness of semiotics has always been to consider meaning production away from what the nature of entities really is; When semiotics is turned to nature however and unhuman entities are allowed to enter into the picture, it soon appears that the words 'discourse' or 'meaning' May be dropped altogether without any danger of going back to naive realism or naive naturalism. It is only because semioticians studied texts -- and literary ones at that -- instead of things that they felt obliged to limit themselves to "meaning." in effect they scientistically believed in the existence of things in addition to meaning (not mentioning their belief in the existence of a good old social context whenever it suited them). but a semiotics of things is easy, One simply has to drop the meaning bit from semiotics..." (1996, p. 376)

Now, it is important to acknowledge that Latour's comments here are not directed at feminist scholarship specifically, but he does seem to be pointing to a sense that contemporary scholarship is limited in its primacy of meaning, which does seem to align with what Karen Barod is saying in the excerpt that Ahmed is critiquing, which makes me wonder not only about Ahmed's motivations but also what the underlying motivation is on the part of barod for arguing that the primacy of language is producing this barrier for feminist theory and scholarship.

Ultimately, I think at this time I'm not entirely sure of the degree to which I align myself with Ahmed's perspective or not, but her article does certainly raise questions about the way that culture, language, and matter are being defined and centered or left out of the scope of analysis for feminist scholarship during the late 20th century, so I think that is something that will require more intentional reflection and attention as continue reading more of that work.

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
Ahmed's intervention here seems to be primarily focused in responding to feminist new material a scholarship that has positioned itself as, ironically, an intervention to earlier feminist scholarship that has been alleged to not offer enough of a critical interrogation of biology and materialism. What Ahmed is seeking to do, however, is to correct what she sees as a fallacy of that very intervention by revisiting a genealogy of feminists work that has taken up issues, particularly of biology, as part of their critical analysis.

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
Overall, her primary argument seems to be that feminist scholars should be cautious of the way that they narrate the concerns that have been the focus of feminist scholarship overtime. More specifically, she is arguing for there to be greater caution and concern with the way that our research concerns are narrated, especially in relationship to the generations of feminist scholarship that predate our work. In the case of matter, for example, she states that our work should involve a careful and critical analysis of not just weather matter has been discussed in prior scholarship, but how it is discussed "in different ways, for different feminisms, over time" (p. 36).

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
She includes several excerpts and descriptions of feminist scholarship overtime that is specifically focused on issues of matter and biology, including Deidre Janson-Smith (1980), Lynda Birke (1986), or the broader body of work from scholars includng Donna Haraway, Evelyn Fox Keller, Emily Martin, Sandra Harding, and Sara Franklin.

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
Her article seems to be largely argumentative and descriptive in nature, though she does so specifically through a detailed literature review.
