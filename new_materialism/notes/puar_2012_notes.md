# Notes for "I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess": Becoming-intersectional in assemblage theory (Puar, J.K., 2012)

## Citation Information
Puar, J.K. (2012). "I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess": Becoming-intersectional in assemblage theory. *philoSOPHIA*, *2*(1), 49-66. https://doi.org/10.1353/phi.2012.a486621

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Opens by referencing previous scholarship where Puar has argued for an assemblage approach to understanding intersectionality, solidly placing her scholarship within the realm of feminist and queer studies.

- She explains that the purpose of the text is interrogate both of these concepts, _intersectionality_ and _assemblage_, assessing them individually in terms of where they might be situated in relation to one another, particularly for the purpose of feminist theory.

- The first section traces the origins of intersectionality theory, starting with Kimberlé Crenshaw's work, leading into a variety of explanations as to the importance of intersectional approaches, as well as considering critiques of intersectionality, including that it reifies the Othering of women of color.

- She takes up critical analysis of intersectionality theory, especially in feminist discourses, drawing on poststructuralist scholarship and critiques of epistemic violence and injustice in doing so.

- She then shifts into a survey of posthumanist and Deleuzian feminist scholars' work to consider how they critique and attempt to dismantle representational politics and ontologies. More specifically, however, she considers how these scholars have taken up or responded to intersectionality, hence her discussion of Haraway's statement that she would prefer to be a cyborg over a goddess.

- She traces new materialism and non-dualistic approaches as a particular strain of posthumanist thought that might offer fruitful reconfigurations of how materiality, semiotics, and identity are understood relationally

- This historicization of intersectionality and assemblage informed approaches to feminist scholarship ultimately results in Puar considering how they might be taken up more explicitly in conversation together, rather than as separate or opposing frameworks

How does Puar, then, like these other scholars, trace the relationship between language and representation and materiality, embodiment, or identity?

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
The major contribution from Puar here is to consider how to intervene in the way that intersectionality, and its perceived ties to representational or identity politics, has led to a perspective that it is incompatible with assemblage theory, despite the meaningful contributions of both theoretical perspectives to feminist scholarship and praxis.

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
She outlines the affordances and limitations of both theoretical perspectives, ultimately arguing that they should not be understood as mutually exclusive or entirely good/bad; instead, she argues in favor of feminist perspectives that build on both intersectional and assemblage informed views:

"Part of the assumption at work in these queries is that representation, and its recognized subjects, is the dominant, primary, or most efficacious platform of political intervention, while a Deleuzian nonrepresentational, non-subject-oriented politics is deemed impossible. Perhaps thesse queries also reveal concerns about how they might be somehow incompatible or even oppositional, despite the fact that intersectionality and assemblage are not analogous in terms of content, utility, or deployment. As analytics, they may not be reconcilable. Yet they need not be oppositional but rather, I argue, frictional" (p. 50).

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
- Tracing genealogies of intersectional or representational / non-representational philosophical thought and feminist theory

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
- Crenshaw's work on intersectionality, especially in the context of legal scholarship and critical race theory
- Work from second and third wave feminists to better account for the full dimensions of marginalized experience
- Traces work from nonrepresentational feminist scholars on matter and the instability of identity (i.e. Barad, Haraway, Grosc, Parisi, Colebrook, etc...)
- Discusses criticisms of Haraway's cyborg manifesto and her invoking of a broad category of "WOC" to place in binary opposition to perceived dominant forms of identity
