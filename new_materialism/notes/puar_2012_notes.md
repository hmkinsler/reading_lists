# Notes for "I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess": Becoming-intersectional in assemblage theory (Puar, J.K., 2012)

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Opens by referencing previous scholarship where Puar has argued for an assemblage approach to understanding intersectionality, solidly placing her scholarship within the realm of feminist and queer studies.

- She explains that the purpose of the text is interrogate both of these concepts, _intersectionality_ and _assemblage_, assessing them individually in terms of where they might be situated in relation to one another, particularly for the purpose of feminist theory.

- The first section traces the origins of intersectionality theory, starting with Kimberlé Crenshaw's work, leading into a variety of explanations as to the importance of intersectional approaches, as well as considering critiques of intersectionality, including that it reifies the Othering of women of color.

- She takes up critical analysis of intersectionality theory, especially in feminist discourses, drawing on poststructuralist scholarship and critiques of epistemic violence and injustice in doing so.

- She then shifts into a survey of posthumanist and Deleuzian feminist scholars' work to consider how they critique and attempt to dismantle representational politics and ontologies. More specifically, however, she considers how these scholars have taken up or responded to intersectionality, hence her discussion of Haraway's statement that she would prefer to be a cyborg over a goddess.

- She traces new materialism and non-dualistic approaches as a particular strain of posthumanist thought that might offer fruitful reconfigurations of how materiality, semiotics, and identity are understood relationally

- This historicization of intersectionality and assemblage informed approaches to feminist scholarship ultimately results in Puar considering how they might be taken up more explicitly in conversation together, rather than as separate or opposing frameworks

How does Puar, then, like these other scholars, trace the relationship between language and representation and materiality, embodiment, or identity?
