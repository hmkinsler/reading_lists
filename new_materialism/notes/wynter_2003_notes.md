### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- This reading is firmly rooted in decolonial scholarship, with Wynter notably being a protege of Carter G. Woodson and connecting her work with scholars such as Fanon and Quijano. Though this may seem as though it is a bit of a departure from "new materialist" perspectives, my primary concern here is to consider how Wynter confronts the subject/object and related dichotomies that have undergirded forms of white supremacy, and where does this intersect with the non-dualist ontology of new materialist scholarship?

- Where are there analogs or potential connections to the way that Puar puts legal, political, or other disciplinary interrogations of power (i.e. intersectionality theory) into conversation with new materialist theories (i.e. assemblages)? How can Wynter's work, and the work of other antiracist and decolonial scholars, be brought into the new materialist fold as well?

- Todd (2016) offers a critique of the uptake of the "ontological turn" in the humanities and social sciences due to a lack of attention to the nondualist ontologies that are not only fundamental to many Indigenous communities and their ontoepistemological perspectives, but that have been systematically and violently surpressed. Knowing this, could a similar critique of new materialist scholarship be made in relationship to the decolonial perspectives of scholars like Wynter?
