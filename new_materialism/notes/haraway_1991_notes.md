# Notes for A cyborg manifesto: Science, technology, and socialist-feminism in the late twentieth century (Haraway, D., 1991)

## Citation Information
Haraway, D. (1991). A cyborg manifesto: Science, technology, and socialist-feminism in the late twentieth century. In *Simians, cyborgs, and women: The reinvention of nature* (pp. 149-182). Routledge.

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This particular work from Haraway is what launched interest in the figure of the cyborg, considering the hybridity of human-machine relations, especially in ways that trouble our understanding of traditional identity categories

- She offers the figure of the cyborg as a political project

- She explores several binarisms, dualities, or distinctions that she feels have or are in the process of breaking down, necessitating the political ontology of the cyborg:
    - "Biological-determinist ideology" (p. 308)
    - "animal-human (organism) and machine" (p. 309)
    - "the boundary between physical and non-physical is very imprecise for us" (p. 309)

- The first section explores the fracturing of identities, as one example in which these boundaries are being broken down, placing her work in the realm of postmodernist and poststructuralist scholarship

- The second section, "the informatics of domination" (p. 316) considers how one might develop connections between previously boundaried or isolated phenomena, things, identities, etc., and the importance of doing this work in the midst of ongoing tendencies to other, marginalize, oppress, and subjugate experiences and phenomena that do not readily submit to capture as information

- Third section, "women in the integrated circuit" (p. 320) considers how resistance to informatics of domination might take place through coalition building, connections across communities, and a recognition of relationally situated experiences and their political implications

- Fourth section, "Cyborgs: a myth of political identity" (p. 322) draws on the work of science fiction writers who Haraway argues offer a theory of the cyborg by considering issues of embodiment, identity and politics, and marginalizations
