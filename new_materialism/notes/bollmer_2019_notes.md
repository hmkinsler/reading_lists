# Notes for Objects and affects (Bollmer, G., 2019)

## Citation Information
Bollmer, G. (2019). Objects and affects. In *Materialist media theory: An introduction* (pp. 141-172). Zed Books. https://doi.org/10.5040/9781501337086

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Given that this is intended to be an introductory text, as evident in the book's title, the chapter opens with an open consideration of what it means to consider objects outside of the historical subject/object dichotomy or dualism that has undergirded much of Western philosophical thought in the last several centuries, prompting his survey and outlining of the trajectory of scholarship on this issue.

- He opens with the perspective of phenomenologists such as Husserl and Heidegger on the problem of subject/object, and the consequences of their perspective for how we understand materiality.

- He then shifts into a discussion of how Ahmed takes up and responds to Husserl and Heidegger's work, and her argument that materiality is "a _process of mattering_, one in which a range of subjects and objects are linked in relation, but in which these relations are inequivalent, even oppositional" (p. 145).

- He states that he starts with these three scholars to open up pathways for a _vital materialist_ approach, which he acknowledges has limitations or risks much like object oriented ontologies (OOO) do. He aligns this interest with vital materialism with the work of Jane Bennett, considering how her and feminist materialist scholarship can enable a productive or generative use of both vital materialist and OOO frameworks.

- He then surveys various definitions for "thing" and "object" as the major matter of concern at the center of these materialist approaches

- He traces the rise of speculative realism and OOO, considering their points of alignment and differences, and their corresponding scholarly genealogical traditions, offering a survey of realist philosophy, for example

- From a closer survey of vital materialist scholarship, he considers several key areas of relevance of focus within this tradition:
    - Shift from anthropocentrism to animism
    - Alterality and totality
    - Affect and the materiality of the brain

- He finally ends in tracing commonalities and points of difference between vital materialist perspectives and OOO

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- Given that this is a more general survey text or introduction to vital materialism and related or distinct traditions, what would it look like to map out the scholarly genealogy of these approaches to objects based on Bollmer's literature review? For example, how is the realist tradition defined, and with which key scholars? How do vital materialists, OOO scholars, and speculative realists take up that tradition differently? Etc.

- Why does affect scholarship find its roots in materialist philosophy? Likewise, how does assemblage theory or vitalist materialism form as distinct frameworks or focuses from a common scholarly or philosophical interest in non-dualism?

- Related to the earlier two questions, it seems in other words, the big picture question is whether or not we can attempt a mapping of the "ontological turn" in the humanities and social sciences, and more specifically, whether we can do this mapping in terms of how this turn took hold in STS and media studies specifically.
