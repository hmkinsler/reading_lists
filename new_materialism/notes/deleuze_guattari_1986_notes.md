#### Body without Organs
For Deleuze and Guattari, the issue of the organism is that it is already organized. It has been determined to have a hierarchy of parts and organs that are already set in a predetermined relation to each other. Whereas the body without organs is full of untethered circulations of flows and intensities that have the potential for a multitude of connections that if we were to put in relationship to the rhizome concept or if we were to understand those two concepts as connected then a rhizomatic approach is much like taking on the analytic perspective of the body without organs. Whereas a structural or arborescent approach would be looking at the level of the organism. But the issue of this being a risky practice is that there are times at which it is helpful to trace issues of power, for example, because people are often socially organized in some way. And so if one just wholly destratifies and does not acknowledge the existence of organization, then that can lead to absolving oneself of the political implications of these hierarchies.
