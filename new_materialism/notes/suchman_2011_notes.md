### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
There are are a surprising amount of connections between this article and that from Tamboukou (2019) and Mitchell (2002), In that all three texts Are primarily focused on methodological issues or the assumptions that underpin a theoretical and analytical frameworks in the context of humanities and social sciences research. In her article, Tamboukou focuses on archival research as something that is processual in nature. her interest in the notion of work to be made, rather than work to be done, suggests a shift away from taking up methods as a given, instead considering how historical researchers are making their work as they conduct it, connecting with the texts of their analysis both within and beyond the boundaries of the archive. Mitchell likewise expresses interest in the contingencies of historical research, arguing in favor of a social theoretical approach that treats historical actors with less isolation, to situate the numerous human and nonhuman agents at play in shaping a given event relationally and enmeshed with each other. Though the other two articles are more distinctly focused on archives and historical narrative writing, essentially work within the disciplinary context of history, Suchman's concern here with the way that our methods and frameworks shape research outcomes suggests an overarching concern in this "ontological turn" of the humanities and social sciences with how dualism or Western onto-epistemologies have affected that work.  

Of course, too, there are several references to the work of John Law, especially his 2004 book _After method: Mess in social science research._ 

This does feel in some ways reminiscent of the way that Susan Leigh Star discusses _articulation work_, which I noted when I read Suchman (2007) on _situated actions_, as well.

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
this piece is meant to act as an ethnomethodological reflection or response to the tendency of STS researchers to overlook how they are implicated not only in the findings that emerged from the various analyses they perform, but the very analytical tools that they apply are themselves objects that come out of the researchers' making. 

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
“The point is that these [research] objects, while arguably relevant to practitioners, are also analytic ones, of our making. I've made them here in a particular way, one which I could defend in relation to my practical and analytical purposes, but which I would also always want to recognize could be otherwise. These objects are, in short, part of a practice, my practice as a researcher and speaker here at this conference, talking to you now. Like all object making, the delineation of a practice is always and irredemiably part _of_ a practice that informs what constitute productive and coherent units of analysis. It is that which makes _us_ responsible and accountable for a research and it's inclusions. and it is that which calls on us to be attentive to our own practices systematic and necessary exclusions, and respectful of its constitutive overflows” (p. 29).

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
She describes her experience  conducting ethnographic research  in the grounds operation room for an international airline, where she observed how workers kept track of airplanes and their operations for departure and arrival in relation to the airline schedule. in particular, she explains how workers were tasked with negotiating Their own observations of the actual work of these departures and arrivals with the constraints of the machine system that is part of this Airline schedule interface. In her description of one such worker, she writes, “Rick’s job is to maintain a consistent relation between an order of events prescribed via the schedule from the national center, and events at the local site” (p. 27)
