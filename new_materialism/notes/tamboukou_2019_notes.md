# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Opens with argument on conceiving of archives _and_ archival researchers as comprising an assemblage

- Offers some exemplary moments of reflection and analysis from her experiences with archival material pertaining to 18th century feminists in Paris, France

- Considers how to interrogate the way that archival research is often positioned as neutral, when the reality is that both archives and the process of selection, analysis, arrangement of materials, interpretation are themselves non-neutral acts that also suggest less of a subject/object binarism to research and consideration of archival methods overall

- Introduces Whitehead's conception of _process philosophy_ and its relevance for archival research

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- The article opens with numerous references to terms employed by D+G in _A thousand plateaus_. Knowing this, where do we parse out her use of these terms, such as when she argues that "the archive is a living organism, a field of forces where events erupt, tracks are mapped, traces are discerned, and new knowledges emerge and crystallize" (para. 1)?

- Similarly, does Tamboukou describe the archive as similar in any way to the notion of _assemblage_ as it is taken up by D+G or Bennett?

- Alternatively, where can we consider how the archive is situated as both an object constructed in part by researchers and also as an object of research? How would Suchman (2011) respond to this problem in the context of STS research focused on the historical collection, storing, organization, classification, and circulation of information? Or, in terms of human interactions with the digital technologies that are part of archival practice?
