# New Material(ist)

## Rationale
This list is built on scholarship in new materialism, particularly as a theoretical and ontological framework that emerged in the late twentieth century as a contestation of the prevalence of dualism and essentialism that pervaded contemporary theory following the linguistic turn and rise of social constructionism. Beginning with foundational work from scholars such as Karen Barad, Manuel DeLanda, and Jane Bennett, the list explores how new materialist thought recenters matter as an active force in shaping the material world. In other words, I consider how new materialism explores connections between matter, agency, and relationality in ways that impact human and nonhuman actors alike.

Such an approach is meaningful in theoretically informing my work for the Fourth Ward Oral History Project, particularly in considering how the neighborhood’s history, culture, and community ties are mediated through archival technologies, including the project’s public-facing digital community archive, and the book scanner that is being developed to digitize records on the neighborhood from the City of Raleigh. Like Tamboukou (2019), I take up scholarship in new materialism as both a theoretical framework and also a model for practice that conceives of the archive or the archival process as an assemblage, a collection of interrelations comprising the researcher, the archival technologies used to collect information, the historical events being depicted in archival records, and more. For this reason, a feminist new materialist perspective is essential in enabling me to interrogate the implications and consequences of my role in compiling, curating, and creating an archive, as my relationship to all aspects of the archival process is entangled with these materials. In other words, I look to this scholarship to consider what is revealed when attention is refocused to the materials that enable or inhibit the documentation and circulation of these historical records.
