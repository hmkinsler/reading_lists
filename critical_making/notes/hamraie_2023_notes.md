# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Drawing on similar principles outlined in their _Crip technoscience manifesto_ (Hamraie & Fritsch, 2019), Hamraie opens by highlighting tensions between crip community practices that leverage and employ digital technologies as sites for connection with the risks of technoableist perspectives that frame technology as a solution to disability.

- From their concerns with the politics of design and their implications for disabled communities, Hamraie argues the need for an intervention into crip perspectives on making: "The politics of disability technology raise important theoretical and empirical questions about the epistemologies, methodologies, and societal implications of design _by_, rather than _for_, disabled people" (p. 304)

- As they describe it, they explore their proposed _crip making_ framework in three ways: "First, I examine controversies surrounding the role of the maker (as author or progenitor) in debates about disability, design, and technology. Then, I examine political-relational and performance-based approaches to disability-led design, looking at the design politics of lived-experience and complicating notions of 'standpoint epistemology' and 'situated knowledge.' Finally, I draw on an example of crip making during the COVID-19 pandemic to highlight the complex politics of access friction and negotiation" (p. 304)

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read

- In what ways does Hamraie's crip making framework continue to push forward the goals outlined in their crip technoscience manifesto?
