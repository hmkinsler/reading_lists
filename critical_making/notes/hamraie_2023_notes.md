# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- Drawing on similar principles outlined in their _Crip technoscience manifesto_ (Hamraie & Fritsch, 2019), Hamraie opens by highlighting tensions between crip community practices that leverage and employ digital technologies as sites for connection with the risks of technoableist perspectives that frame technology as a solution to disability.

- From their concerns with the politics of design and their implications for disabled communities, Hamraie argues the need for an intervention into crip perspectives on making: "The politics of disability technology raise important theoretical and empirical questions about the epistemologies, methodologies, and societal implications of design _by_, rather than _for_, disabled people" (p. 304)

- As they describe it, they explore their proposed _crip making_ framework in three ways: "First, I examine controversies surrounding the role of the maker (as author or progenitor) in debates about disability, design, and technology. Then, I examine political-relational and performance-based approaches to disability-led design, looking at the design politics of lived-experience and complicating notions of 'standpoint epistemology' and 'situated knowledge.' Finally, I draw on an example of crip making during the COVID-19 pandemic to highlight the complex politics of access friction and negotiation" (p. 304)

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
Seeks to respond to and dispel discourses of technoableism and technosaviorism that position disabled people as in need of "fixing" or outside assistance rather than acknowledgig the design expertise frequently employed by disabled people as a world-building practice.

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
The author looks at a "crip nightlife party" (p. 312) that took place via Zoom during the COVID-19 pandemic as an "experiment" in crip making. The party involved a performance from a DJ as attendees in other Zoom "boxes" danced, listened outdoors, or were sat before their screens, with ASL interpretation, descriptions of music and conversation unfolding in the chat, and the author providing visual descriptions of the event. They describe some of the challenges that went into the design of the event, like figuring out how to both provide visual description of what was unfolding while not interfering with the music. the ultimately illustrate that this is an act of Crip making precisely because it is seeking to meet the material needs of the people who are participating in the event who are seeking to engage with each other collectively and socially at a time where physical distancing and isolation as a result of the threat of the pandemic illustrated a need for "movement, celebration, and kinship in the midst of grief" (p. 312). Critically, she notes, this illustrates the collectiveness of crip making practices, especially as part of mutual aid.

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
Synthesizing of Hamraie's previous work on crip technoscience (also highlighted in Hamraie & Fritsch, 2019), design justice (Costanza-Chock, 2020), and critical making
