# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The author looks at a Twitter bot on a Raspberry Pi micro computer that registered as a member of the MLA and Co presented a paper alongside the author at the MLA convention in 2017. In order to account for the ecological and material costs of the bot, Burgess attempts to understand life cycle reports, which are developed by auditors who seek to account for the cost of any given electronic component. She employs Latour's "compilation work" as a theoretical framework for understanding the complexities and limitations of such lifecycle reports.

- She argues that Latour describes compilation work doesn't mean to understand the various links between computational materials , machines, and our social practices. In this case, Burgess attempts to trace the trajectory of the various materials that make up the hardware of "Anna", the Twitterbot. This could include the silicone and rare earth minerals that make up the CPU, but this is complicated by the bots role in the Internet of Things, Through its connection to data centers and servers and the Internet in order to receive and transmit information.

- One portion of the paper looks at various stages of Anna's development, including the coding in Python that allowed for the bot to tweet strings of anacoluthons fr a rhetorical style class, and another round of development that enabled the bot to respond during the Q&A of the conference session. Ultimately, Burgess writes that "Techically, then, Anna is three separate soft entities (two Twitter apps and one web app), as well as numerous hard ones (the Pi, remote servers, communication networks, the conference room); and yet all these combine to produce the performed identity that is Anna Coluthon" (p. 81)

- From there, Burgess begins to account for the various costs of the bot, whether the silicon in its chip, the embodied energy used in its production, the carbon emissions associated with its manufacturing, energy expenditures associated with the data centers and servers on which it relies, coltan, and even the social consequences and discourses surrounding issues of the environment and the ecology of computing.

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
here, by drawing on theoretical frameworks offered by sociologists and historians of science, burgess argues that digital humanists need to more fully account for the ecological and political or social consequences of using the media and technologies that are employed in order to conduct that research. Whether that has to do with the use of microcontrollers in order to collect and process data, or in the production of potential future waste using desktop manufacturing , in the use of cloud data platforms in order to store and transmit information via the Internet, Her case study of the Twitter bot offers just one example of what it means to try to more fully account for the consequences of digital humanities work.

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
I found it especially interesting that much of the compilation work of the article involved sorting through a variety of reports and studies related to the manufacturing, transport, and sourcing of the materials on which computing hardware relies, as well as the ongoing costs of Internet infrastructure in the form of data centers. As she notes, this compilation work is necessarily incomplete, as each reporting agency or source that is attemting to measure these environmental and material costs is going to be shaped by its institutional context and is fraught with various kinds of bias and limitations, and yet, These are the sources of information that we have to work with into question and to attempt to contextualize when we were thinking about the stakes of materials and energy consumption for Digital Humanities projects .

### How: What methods and theories are used to make the intervention or argument and support it?
One of the major theoretical frameworks that the 8 article relies on comes from Bruno Latour, especially from his "On interobjectivity", Where he argues that compilation work, or a method of describing the multiply connected, messy, and relational positioning of ourselves with the computational, environmental, and machinic world around us, is a methodological approach that affords us the ability to better understand the roles of the technological and material world in the dynamic unfolding of structuration effects.
