# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The authors draw on their experiences attempting to develop 3D pritned replicas of ivory busts for the Art Gallery of Ontario, attempting to make the plastic pieces feel more representative of the ivory material of their original counterparts

- They draw on their observations to consider how STS and digital humanities scholars might approach the material implications for critical making work in institutional settings such as archives, museums, galleries, libraries, etc.

- They reflect on the digital-material tensions and issues that are frequently raised in digital humanities work with such cultural institutions

- They ultimately offer some guidance for how digital humanities scholars might make use of the affordances of 3D printing as a method for digitization of physical archival materials

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- How might this article be understood in terms of the ecological and material concerns that relate to the production of critical making projects, especially in the context of 3D printing?

- How has scholarship on this issue changed in recent years, especially as 3D printing materials, 3D scanning and VR technologies, and the like have continued to develop and be employed in DH work?

- The title, "thinking as handwork," suggests an embodied-epistemological perspective to knowledge production, which calls back to Ratto's (2011) investment in the critical making framework as a model for conceptualization through direct and active engagement with materials and technologies. How does this article continue to build on, trouble, or respond to his original description of that framework?
