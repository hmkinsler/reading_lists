# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article is primarily focused on introducing the critical making Responsibility framework As a means for evaluating Responsibility in grassroots innovation. They Begin with a literature review that highlights the shortcomings of previous frameworks, especially for the lack of scientific focus on ethics as it relates to maker practices.
- They highlight the necessity for ethical frameworks for making an innovation, especially given the documented issue of technoliberal, techno solutionist, and ideologically colonial discourses that frequently pervade the maker movement , including in university and research spaces.
- This framework emerged out of specific goals of the Critical Making Consortium, where researchers were specifically working together to establish frameworks for evaluating the ethics and issues of responsibility that are specific to maker practices in research and education, especially given the complexities of grass root innovation processes in various contexts globally, including in the global North and the Global South. The consortium Specifically was responding to the existing Responsible Research and Innovation (RRI) Framework, Which was designed with a greater focus toward institutionally sponsored and supported innovation practices, which tend to be more aligned with the norms and social structuring of the global North, and less appropriate for communities in the global South or grassroots innovation contexts .
- The critical Making Responsibility Framework, then, is specifically focused on considering the socio-ethical relationships between maker(s) and the communities they are in partnership along several dimensions:
    - Spaces and Strategies: "Arenas for grass roots innovations, norms, expectations" (p. 659)
    - Pathways: "Plurality of new pathways for socio-technical innovations" (p. 659)
    - Contexts: "Circumstances, socio-technical landscapes open 'windows of opportunities'" (p. 659)
    - Framings: "Collective identity, shared meanings, interpretations, narratives" (p. 659)
- These four dimensions are then paired with several of the RRI competencies to form a matrix for the critical Making Responsibility framework, Including the following RRI competencies: 
    - Anticipation: "...systematic thinking which Aims to increase resilience of communities and helps to recognize and create opportunities for challenging the existing state of the art with novel social and technical innovations" (p.663)
    - Reflexivity: "...deliberate rethinking of how one's activity's encounter and reflect the social norms and conventions and potentially challenge or strengthen existing social power relations, division of Labor and costs and benefits or whether it causes potential risks for other people or ecological environment" (p. 663)
    - Inclusiveness: "...the need to include multiple voices and stakeholders in the innovation and making to bring in legitimacy and to provide an opportunity for stakeholders to express their concerns and opinions about the direction of activities" (p. 663)
    - Responsiveness: "...the ultimate aim of the three previous RRI principles: to increase the capacity of researchers in science and innovation system to be responsive for social challenges related to their research" (p. 663)
