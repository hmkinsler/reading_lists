# Reading Notes

### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article looks at the development of a research method in partnership with a Township in the Columbian Caribbean region as a case study in decolonial methodology and participatory design 
- data for the paper includes field notes, audio and video recordings of meetings, participant generated media, such as photos and videos
- Becerra was especially interested in understanding their positionality as a researcher who is associated with academic institutions in relation to School teachers who live in a rural community
- Much of the findings center around the importance of understanding the local communities epistemological frameworks, lived experiences, and embodied practices , with Becerra arguing that a collaborative and decolonial research methodology begins with a re centering of these local community knowledge practices
- I found it really interesting that there is a section in which each of the research Co designers are described in terms of their background, providing biographies in the same way that we typically see when somebody is submitting to A academic journal, for example
- Importantly, there is a section in which Becerra explains that these research Co participants were not only providing data in collaboration with the researcher, but they also were active participants in analysis of this data, which I find to be a shift from other participatory action research or participatory design research that I have encountered in the past 
- Overall, through these practices in communal walking through the community, Crowdsourcing of notes and data to then be analyzed collaboratively, in a sense that every participant is an active researcher seeking to confront the design challenge that they are seeking to overcome, Becerra illustrates how integrating members of the community who are stakeholders in this research problem allows for a decolonial methodology through which the stakeholders more fully take on the role of Co designer.

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
- Overall, I actually found this to be an incredibly helpful article because Becerra highlights a lot of the same anxieties intentions that I have experienced while working on the 4th Ward oral History project. In particular, I found that I have grapple with bridging what I see as a disconnect between my as a member of the 4th Word community who attends community meetings and has been integrated into that community in a very familiar way With my membership in a higher education institution. It can be very challenging to know how to bring communal and decolonial ethos out of just the relational and collective experiences that I have amongst 4th Word residents into the actual method , analysis, and theorizing that I engage in as part of my research work. This is where I really see the remarkable insight that Becerra offers in this article, Explaining how local epistemological and ontological perspectives were taking in consideration not just to grant "participation" in a research design project, but also as the driving force behind the methodological choices themselves. For example, Alice Becerra was coming to know what the lived experiences of the community members were, these Co researchers suggested engaging in community walks together because of sense that it would be necessary to be present with the land in order to communally have the experiential knowledge that is necessary to be able to Co theorize and Co analyze together. 

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
- I could definitely see this being a model for thinking about how to describe some of the research work that I've done with the 4th word community . For example, there have been times where I have collected oral history interviews in front of locations that residents used to live in or would frequent in the 4th Ward, especially around West South St. In another instance, Rosa, clara, and myself helped lead a community bus tour that was attended by local residents and city and county officials so that they could learn more about the neighborhood, and that feels like an example of extending beyond collaborative or community based research to then specific pedagogical or public history practice that likewise draws on those local community epistemologies to position those residents as experts or educators who understand that one would need to be situated in the space of the 4th word neighborhood itself in order to actually be able to fully capture the knowledge that they are sharing. 

- I did struggle a bit with one section of the article where Becerra discusses the concept of an _oídora_, a listener, Especially in the context of a researcher being somebody who listens to the community. At the same time, there are institutional limitations that decolonial researchers are facing, and there are existing power asymmetries that shape who is granted the legitimacy and authority to publish or share the findings that emerge from listening with and to these communities . There seems to be something here that I haven't fully resolved in terms of this tension of being somebody who can be thoroughly embedded in the community, Who can engage in this thoughtful reflection, while still being tasked with making these findings visible and sharing them on behalf of a community in the context of scholarly publications.

## Intervention, Argument, Evidence
### Intervention: What is the intervention that the scholar is making, and where is that intervention being made? In other words, how do you place that intervention relative to existing disciplinary conversations that are taking place?
- From my perspective, it seems that Becerra it's drawing on participatory action research to consider not what it means just to design with a community but to extend that sense of design to the very research process itself , from designing the methodology and data collection practices, to iteratively and communally refining the analytical and theoretical frameworks that are being employed to understand that data, and collaboratively designing texts that present the findings of their research together.

### Argument: What are the core argument(s) being made in the text?
- similar to the intervention that is being made in this article, I think Becerra's argument here is that a decolonial research method itself requires not imposing Expectations for research outcomes or the means by which those outcomes will be achieved onto a community . Instead, she argues that the goals and methods of research themselves should come organically in collaboration with the research community , drawing on their local epistemologies and ontologies in part of that research design work.

### Evidence: What evidence is used to support the argument(s)?
- Becerra walks through her work with a Caribbean Colombian community , especially several teachers at a school in that community, illustrating how community members shaped the methods, analysis, and theoretical work of this research project .
