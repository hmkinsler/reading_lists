# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article is focused on arguing that Community-Engaged Research (CER) Requires a focus on cultural humility as a foundational value as a "personal precondition for socially just, decolonial CER practice" (p. 12), esecially drawing on the Inuit cultural practice of _nalukataq_ as a model for engaging in this cultural humility work
- They highlight just how pertinent the need for cultural humility as a value in CER is Given the long standing history of research extraction from indigenous communities in the Arctic, the consequences of which are exacerbated in the midst of the current environmental crisis that we face 
- They acknowledge that though recent efforts have been made to begin to remedy or prevent the harms of such research practices, models for what constitutes respect are often fraught as they define respect from the social and epistemological standpoints of outside researchers coming into these communities, rather than being defined by the values of the communities themselves
- For this reason, they look at a specific context within the NANA region, considering how the NANA Regional Elders Council provide guidance about the meaning and importance of humility:
    "Speak only the truth about your deeds.
    Others who have witnessed your deed will speak for you.
    Never embellish, as people will lose their respect when the truth is known.
    Bragging is an embarrassment.
    It is wrong to pass judgment on others.
    Never think that you are better than anyone else.
    Elders and parents should teach and model humility.
    Effective leadesr practice humility" (p. 14)
- They look to the cooperative practice of _nalukataq_ as an activity that requires careful coordinaton amongst participants to maintain balance as a metaphor for the need for balanced relationships between researchers and community partners
- Informed by the perspective of the NANA Regional Elders Council and lessons from _nalukataq_, the authors outline several tenents for cultural humility for scholars to be informed by as they engage in CER:
    - "Listening to the caller: Respecting and submitting to community leadership" (p. 16)
    - "Setting your feet: Knowing yourself and adjusting to community needs" (p. 16)
    - "Pulling equally: Accepting your role and _avoiding_ manipulation of the project" (p. 17)
    - "Staying in sync: Maintaining connections and trust" (p. 17)

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- In my own cultural understanding, humility specifically refers to the ability of a person to recognize their abilities and limitations and be careful to engage in fair, honest assessment of those abilities and limitations. Given this, how do The authors offer cultural humility as a way to understand the limitations of researchers in spaces where their cultural norms, values, and practices may be different from those of the communities that they come to contact with? In other words, How does cultural humility enabled researchers to acknowledge those gaps in productive ways so that they can mitigate risks or harms and instead work more closely in alignment with their partner communities?
- With a cultural humility approach, How might researchers make sense of ways to identify and respond to instances where they do overstep in their work with communities?
- How does a specific focus on informed cultural humility take shape in differnt local contexts, such as the Fourth Ward, for example?
