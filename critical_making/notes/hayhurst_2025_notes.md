### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article specifically focuses on decolonial feminist approaches to digital participatory action research specifically in a rural community in Nicaragua, exploring their mobility and cycling practices along axes of gender
- The author explains how They are informed by several decolonial research practices, including recognizing the community that they work with as active Co researchers who are shaping and designing the research process alongside them
- The author is specifically partnering with a local NGO That focuses on empowering girls and women through sports, especially cycling, and the methodology involved conducting a workshop with Co researchers to train them on the use of Gopros which were GPS enabled and then used to record the CO researchers bike rides, after which there were semi structured interviews wherein the Co researcheries offered additional explanation and analysis about the footage that they captured. Furthermore, participants sketched out analytical responses a map of locations through which they biked, and participants work together to develop multimodal colleages across which they identify key themes .
- One of the major themes that resulted from the analysis interrogating issues of power and as women were biking through potentially risky condition , whether through remote places, areas where men may be drinking or fishing, unfamiliar areas, and the like. By drawing the footage and reflections of the CO researchers, It became clear how their local and embodied knowledge as women navigating these spatial environments was key.
