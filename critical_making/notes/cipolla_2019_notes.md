# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- This article covers a course that proposed and then developed With the intention of embodying a feminist maker pedagogy, one that brings together aspects of classrooms, maker spaces, and laboratories into a singular course In which students are encouraged to engage in speculative design and generation that moves beyond mere critical analysis of socio technical issues to consider how feminist design and maker practices enable the envisioning of alternative features
- In this case, this was a techno science studies course, and the author is interested in a feminist maker pedagogy that specifically invests itself in breaking down disciplinary boundaries
- She illustrates some of the affordances of specifically approaching critical and feminist perspectives on technology through maker practices, considering hegemonic disciplinary paradigms that restrict who can be considered as a legitimate scientists or technologists , and what it means to resist those paradigms by embracing more democratic approaches to science and technology studies, shifting understandings of knowledge authority and legitimacy in STS, And confronting diversity issues that have historically pervaded STEM education 
- In particular, she frames the course in terms of tinkering in feminist technoscience, Asking how a Tinkering ethos democratizes students access to STS
