# Notes for Toward a composition made whole (Shipka, J., 2011)

## Citation Information
Shipka, J. (2011). *Toward a composition made whole*. University of Pittsburgh Press.

# Reading Notes
## SQR3
### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas

- The introduction for the book sets forth the stakes for Shipka's argument , especially her concern that recent work in writing, rhetoric, and composition studies has been moving toward a collapsing of terms like multimodality, intertextuality, multimedia, and media rich as stand-ins for texts that are in some way, shape, or form technological or digital. Here, Is where Shipka outlines her interest in developing a more substantial or full account of multimodal composition, both in terms of the process in composing takes place, and in terms of the texts that emerge from that process.

- Chapter one explores two arguments:
    - That composition studies must necessarily shift toward an account of composition beyond written discourse alone.
    - That this shift toward theorizing and researching composition must be careful "against facilitating changes that result in the substitution of one set of sign systems, technologies, and limitations for another or that privilege certain ways of knowing, learning, and composing while denigrating or downplaying the value of othes" (p. 14)

- Chapter two "Examines how a sociocultural approach to communicative practice provides us with ways of attending to the social and individual aspects of composing processes without losing sight of the wide variety of genres, sign systems, technologies that composers routinely employ while creating texts" (p. 14). Here, she introduces a mediated action framework that draws on James Wertsch's scholarship to focus analysis on "the _individual(s)-interacting-with-mediational-means_" (p. 14).

-  Chapter three demonstrates the applicability of the mediated action framework to two composing process studies, illustrating how composition is situated within a complex mesh of related activities and actors that influence the composition process.

- Chapter four extends this framework further, providing a pedagogical approach that refuses predetermination of the means by which students compose while still developing their rhetorical awareness: 

    "The framework highlights the interconnectedness of systems of production, distribution, reception, and circulation by providing students with complex tasks and activities that require them to consider how the contexts in which texts participate shape the way those texts are received and responded to" (p.15).

She analyzes two vignettes of working with first-year composition students to illustrate the efficacy of this framework and pedagogical approach.

- Chapter five continues in this line of framework development, with a specific focus on how to assess and respond to multimodal composition without maintaining a sole focus on digital texts or multimodal pieces that make use of new or emerging technologies. Here, a great emphasis is placed on student's role in the evaluative process, carrying through a current of investment on student autonomy and active participation in shaping the composition process from start to end throughout this book.

### Query: Write questions to consider as you read
- How does the time in which this book was written and published, in the 2010s, shape the intervention that Shipka is seeking to make, especially in terms of a shift toward multimodal and digital composition practices in writing classrooms? How have we seen this interest shape first-year-composition since?

- How are the frameworks outlined in the text relevant for other classes in which composition work is taking place, beyond the scope of classes that are explicitly in rhetoric and composition?

- Given the pedagogical focus of this book, how can we draw on Shipka's framework for making sense of the process and multimodal engagement that is relevant for critical making scholarship? How do scholars like Shivers-McNair and Gollihue take up the argument outlined by Shipka in their work on critical making?
