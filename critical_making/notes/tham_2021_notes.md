### Survey: Skim the text and identify main ideas
- The importance of a design thinking approach for pedagogy in technical and professional communication, especially at a time where there increasing interest in multimodal composition
- Tham surveys the evolution of design research and scholarship, including from architecture and art to programming, engineering, composition, etc.
- Specifically highlights the design thinking model that has been adapted from the Stanford model, which includes the following steps:
    - Empathize
    - Define
    - Ideate
    - Prototype
    - Test
- He argues that design thinking is a natural fit for technical communication pedagogy and research or scholarship, especially given the fields close relationship to research and participatory design , human centered design, UX design, et cetera 
- He especially feels that design thinking is a useful framework for a more embodied, or hands on pedagogical approach and technical communication courses, calling students attention to the materiality of design
- Argues that design thinking is especially important for confronting wicked problems
- In the second chapter, Tham surveys scholarship on maker culture and the rise of maker spaces in universities and colleges , He then looks at three different university maker spaces as case studies to understand how maker pedagogy can show up in various contexts, and what can be learned from those case studies in considering the integration of design thinking practices or processes in technical communication pedagogy
- The third chapter specifically focuses on the efficacy of design thinking and maker pedagogies for confronting social problems or social justice issues in technical communication, pointing to calls from technical communication scholars in recent years about the need for confronting social issues to be a focus of the field
- Highlights different possibilities for social innovation through design 
- He then extends this interest for social innovation or social justice through design to a pedagogical approach that equips students with the tools to engage in social advocacy through design as well 
- In the fourth chapter, where he is focused on this intersection between design, pedagogy, and social justice, Tham offers a variety of assignments that can be used to walk students through each stage of the design thinking process
- In the fifth chapter, Tham Considers the emphasis on collaboration that is frequently seen in design scholarship or scholarship about design thinking and the affordances that this explicitly collaborative approach offers to technical communicators
- In the final chapter, Tham brings together all aspects of his prior argument to make the case a technical communicators that their role is not just to document or explain designs that have been developed to make them accessible or usable to the general public, but that technical communicators through familiarity with the social sciences and humanities, their expertise and composition processes, and their ability to learn from and communicate highly technical information, can actively engage in the process of design themselves to confront social issues

### Recite: Consider what you want to remember, and put that into your own words
Overall, I think the main crux of Tham's argument is that technical communicators are often thought of in terms of working with written or oral texts and confronting technical problems through their commutative expertise, But when we think about maker practices or look at design thinking and critical design scholarship , we are able to see a lot more avenues for technical communicators to facilitate in addressing complex socio technical issues through multimodal, material engagement with those issues. What really stands out to me about technical communications scholarship, then, In terms of how Tham seems to be describing it, it almost seems like their work is very applied. In other words, when we think about professional communication it seems like the idea is that these scholars are entering into industry spaces or working on industry projects , and it is through understanding of concepts and rhetoric or linguistics or principles of communication that they are able to help manage groups of people, facilitate the movement of information between them, or synthesize disparate and complex ideas to help bring things together and move a project forward or bring that information together in a way that helps to address or respond to or reveal some kind of technical issue. What I'm not entirely sure of, however, is the degree to which technical communication feels particularly disciplinarily bounded. It certainly seems to be the case that engineers and students in architecture and design are not necessarily going to be confronted with the same kinds of questions about communication and meaning that are more frequently a matter of concern for humanists , But I also am not sure at what point our work as technical communicators become so interdisciplinary that what we're really advocating for is the integration of humanistic thinking for designers as opposed to design linking for humanists. I think part of my uncertainty here comes from my own anecdotal observations and experiences working for or with industry partners, public officials , and non profit organizations. In those spaces, i'm often thought of as a kind of jack of all trades. I have some facility with principals of modeling and design because of my fiance's technical work, But I have also had to develop graphic design skills, learn to write in a variety of contexts and genres , and I've even taken up some basic principles for programming and physical computing or working with microcontrollers for my own design projects. At some point, The ideal feels as though it would be meaningful to devote technical training to understanding of these issues. While I may be a communicator who is able to understand the operation or social and commutative dynamics of a given business or nonpro and its goals, at some point if I am trying to understand the implications of design thinking on the development of projects that are being put together by technicians, engineers, programmers, and the like, I wonder where the glass ceiling ends up being located? at what point would a technical communicator ideally bridge their humanistic perspectives with the kind of technical and technological expertise that say, an engineer acquires? alternatively, What kinds of issues are we running into when the ability to facilitate communication is largely relegated to the domain of technical communication scholars? I think what I'm ultimately confronted with here is a problem of interdisciplinarity, as it almost feels as though technical communicators become quasi engineers or quasi programmers or quasi architects and quasi designers who may not specialize to the fullest extent in the technical areas and expertise that is typically associated with those domains of work and scholarship but nonetheless bring forth a very particularly specialized form of expertise as it pertains to communication. This may be a bit of a pedantic response to much of what Tham is covering in this book, but this call for greater concern with multimodal composition and principles of design or design thinking certainly leads me to imagine the pedagogical or methodological and theoretical affordances or opportunities that might be opened up through engaging with prototyping and maker practices that are certainly connected very much to related disciplines in engineering, architecture, and design .

### Relate: Identify connections or links to other readings and scholarship
It definitely seems like Tham offers a lot of thoughtful commentary about the benefits of multimodality, which connects well to Shipka (2011). 

I also have been thinking a lot about the rise in popularity on social media for cyber decks and cyber deck projects . A huge community on Tik Tok, for example, is starting to come together to talk about how to make use of Raspberry Pies and ESP32 boards or Arduinos in order to develop personalized technologies. What is especially interesting about this rise in content about DIY technologies is that the people who are participating are often coming in with little to no experience with programming and hardware design . All the same, there is a robust sense of the importance of design in shaping these users' motivations for developing a Cyberdeck project. For example, I've come across dozens of videos from people who are interested in developing their own cameras, MP3 players, or personalized screen displays. In these instances, users are not starting necessarily from a question of the principles underlying programming, and they are instead thinking about the potential to develop a technology that, through its personalization or customizability, is able to solve some kind of unique problem or meet their particular use case. It is from there That these users are seeking out the kind of technical expertise that is required in order to actually put these ideas into production. In other words, It seems like there is some kind of gap that's being reduced for this community between the kinds of specialized and technical information that they feel like they would need in order to accomplish developing a project and their ability to think through principles of design that render their motivation to seek out that information. 

This feels especially important at a time where technological decay or discarding an obsolescence is a serious ecological concern, Not to mention the increasing amount of security vulnerabilities that are being identified through the dissemination of Wi-fi and Bluetooth enabled technologies in the Internet of Things . These users are turning to Linux distributions for the sake of ensuring stronger security protocols for their devices, They are looking at custom phone operating systems that allow them greater control over their phone's features, and they are learning more about principles of cyber security and networking to regain autonomy over their own data and technological devices. it is For these reasons that I think Tham is really pointing to the cutting edge of technical communication scholarship in this book. At a Time where Surveillance is often thought of as a given , more and more people are being subjected to authoritarian violence, and anthropogenic climate change Is putting more and more communities at risk of infrastructural breakdown and natural disasters, It seems only natural for technical communicators to think about design and making as essential for developing the kinds of embodied , material, and technological skills that will enable us to respond to these incredibly pertinent sociopolitical issues.
