1. Synthesis/mapping a conversation. Critical making has been theorized as everything from a pedagogical method for technological literacy (Ratto, Hertz) to an embodied, relational practice (Shivers-McNair) to one strand within a broader decolonial methodology (Escobar, Tunstall, Winschiers-Theophilus et al.). Map how this conversation has shifted across your list — from its foundational framing as bridging conceptual and material exploration toward its more recent framing as a site of care, repair, and decolonial co-construction. Where do these strands converge, and where do they pull in different directions?

2. Case study application. Using at least five readings from your list, analyze the book scanner project you are developing for the Fourth Ward Oral History Project as a work of critical making. What does it mean to design this technology in partnership with a community that has already organized itself (the Fourth Ward Historic Neighborhood Association) around resisting erasure by prior infrastructural interventions (urban renewal)? Where might your positionality as a maker-scholar complicate or clarify this partnership?

3. Dissertation feasibility/framework/contribution. Explain how critical making functions as the methodological anchor of your dissertation project, in conversation with new materialism as your theoretical framework. Drawing on Escobar's pluriverse, Costanza-Chock's design justice, and Hamraie's crip making, defend your methodological choices for the Fourth Ward project and articulate what intervention this work makes in critical making scholarship.

4. Compare/contrast theoretical perspectives. Compare Hamraie and Fritsch's "crip technoscience" with Shivers-McNair's "relational rhetorics" as two distinct ways of theorizing the maker's body and its relations to material, tool, and community. What does each framework foreground that the other risks eliding, and how might they be productively synthesized in a single methodological approach?

5. Define/clarify key themes. Define critical making and trace how the concept of the "maker-scholar" complicates traditional notions of scholarly objectivity. Draw on at least four readings spanning your Foundations and Embodiment sections to explain how situatedness (Haraway), tinkering (Cipolla), and identity/power in the makerspace (Gollihue) each contribute to this redefinition.

6. Gaps and future directions. Identify a limitation or underexplored area in the critical making scholarship on your list — for instance, its engagement (or lack thereof) with AI or automated technologies, or tensions between individual maker-scholar accounts and collective/decolonial community-based making. What future research, including your own, might address this gap?

7. Exemplar studies. Choose three studies from your list that you consider methodological or theoretical exemplars of critical making scholarship (e.g., Ratto 2011, Costanza-Chock 2020, Sipos & Åkerman 2023). For each, explain what it does well methodologically and theoretically, and what makes it a model for your own critical making practice.
